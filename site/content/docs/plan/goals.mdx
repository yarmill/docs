Each goal holds a set of **key results** — measurable outcomes with a start, current, and target value — so progress stays visible season-long.

## Where to find Goals

**Open Goals from the main menu.** As an **athlete**, you'll see your own goals straight away. As a **coach**, pick a **group** and then an athlete to work with their goals — or open your own Goals to set objectives for yourself.

Goals are grouped by season, each group with its own count: **Current season**, **Past season**, and **Other** — everything older than last season. Old objectives stay out of the way without being lost.

## Create a goal

A goal is a single objective ("Lift 150 kg on bench press," "Reach the World Championship final"). Give it a name and some context, then break it into key results.

<Frame caption="The parts of a goal: a title and description, its attributes, and a table of key results.">
  <img src="/images/goals/goal-anatomy.png" alt="A goal made up of a title and description, a row of attributes (state, season, priority, supervisors, category), and a key results table with start, current, target values and a target date." />
</Frame>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Goals and pick the person">
    Select the athlete whose goal you're creating — or yourself, as a coach.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a new goal">
    Select **New goal** in the action bar at the bottom of the goal panel — or just press
    **N**. An empty goal appears, ready to edit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in the goal">
    Give it a title and description, then set its attributes (see below) and add key
    results. There's no Save button — changes apply immediately and are logged under
    **Activity**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Frame caption="A new goal opens ready to edit: a placeholder title, a Describe it field, and buttons for its attributes.">
  <img src="/images/goals/new-goal.png" alt="A new, empty goal with placeholder title, a Describe it field, and buttons to set state, season, priority, supervisors, and category." />
</Frame>

### Goal fields

<ParamField path="Title" required>
  The objective, in a few words — for example, "Lift 150 kg on bench press."
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Description">
  Free-text context: what success looks like and by when.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="State" default="Not started">
  The goal's status: **Not started**, **On track**, **Off track**, **Completed**,
  **Canceled**, or **Failed**.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Season" default="Current season">
  The season the goal belongs to. Goals are grouped by season in the list.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Priority" default="Low">
  **Low**, **Medium**, or **High** — set from the **!** button. It shows as one to three
  exclamation marks, on the goal and in the list.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Supervisors">
  The coaches who follow the goal. Use **Add supervisors** to pick them from a searchable
  list.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Category">
  A two-level label — a category and its subcategory (for example, *Fitness → Strength* or
  *Health → Recovery*). Set it from **Set category**. The available categories are
  **configured for your team**.
</ParamField>

<Frame caption="The category menu: a top-level category and its subcategory.">
  <img src="/images/goals/category-picker.png" alt="The category menu open, showing top-level categories such as Performance, Fitness, Personal, Technical, Health, and Conditions, each with a submenu." />
</Frame>

## Add key results

**A key result is a measurable outcome** that tells you whether the goal is being met. Add as many as the goal needs — each row in the table is one outcome.

There are no sub-steps or milestones; key results are the only way to break a goal down.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Name the key result">
    Type into the inline **Name the key result** row at the bottom of the table to add a row.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the values">
    Fill in **Start value**, **Current value**, and **Target value**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set a target date">
    Select **Set date** and choose when the key result should be reached.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Key result fields

<ParamField path="State">
  The key result's status — the same six states as the goal. It's the table's first column.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Name" required>
  The measurable outcome — for example, "Train at least 3× per week."
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Start value">
  Where the athlete began. Free text — a number (`0`) or a phrase (`unknown`).
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Current value">
  Where the athlete is now. Updated **manually** as progress is made.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Target value">
  The value that counts as success — for example, `140kg` or `3`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Target date">
  When the key result should be reached.
</ParamField>

## Track progress

**Values are updated manually**, so keep them current as the athlete progresses — the conversations around a goal are only as good as its latest numbers. Update each key result's **current value**, and move the goal's **state** as the picture changes. The progress fraction counts achieved key results against the total.

**Every change is logged automatically** — a new value, a state change, a moved target date, a rename — under **Activity**, with who made it and when.

To remove a goal, use the **trash icon** in the action bar and confirm — deleting is
permanent, so the goal and its key results go with it.

## Comments and final evaluations

**Use Activity to leave a comment at any time.** Comments are plain text, timestamped, and a good way for an athlete and coach to talk through progress in context.

When a goal wraps up, add a **final evaluation** — a short closing reflection on how it went. Both the athlete and a coach can add their own.

**A final evaluation can't be added until the goal reaches a final state** — **Completed**,
**Canceled**, or **Failed**. Before that the box is there but greyed out, so set the closing
state first and then evaluate. The box only appears for the people expected to write one: the
athlete and the goal's supervisors. Comments in **Activity**, by contrast, work at any point in
the goal's life.

<Tip>Goals are at their best in pre- and post-season conversations: set them together at
the start of the season, and evaluate them together at the end.</Tip>

## Check a goal is properly set up

**The checklist icon in the action bar opens Verification** — a check of the goal against your
team's guidelines for writing one. Each item is marked:

- **Red** — required, and still missing.
- **Amber** — recommended, and still missing.
- **Green** — done.

The icon carries a **dot badge** while anything is outstanding, so you can see a goal needs
attention without opening the panel. **Open guidelines** at the bottom links out to your team's
own reference document.

<Info>What counts as required or recommended — such as a minimum number of key results, or
whether a description is expected — comes from your team's configured guidelines and differs
between teams.</Info>

## Export goals

**The download icon at the top of the goals list opens Export options** — a short menu of
documents Yarmill can generate from the athlete's goals.

**Export goals** is the one every team has: choose the **season** and select **Export**. What
comes out is the athlete's goals and key results for that season, in a form you can take into a
meeting or attach to a review.

<Info>The rest of the menu is **configured per team** — instances add the exports their
federation or club paperwork needs, so yours may list different entries, or only *Export goals*.</Info>

## See a whole group's goals

**As a coach, select Entire Group** to see the whole squad on one screen. Every athlete is a row — each goal a rounded pill colored by its state, followed by a small circle per key result, so a row reads as "how many goals, and how is each one going." Athletes with no goals still appear as a labeled row.

Hover a goal for a quick summary — its title, season, progress fraction, and category — or select it to open the full view. A fast way to scan a squad before a planning meeting or review.

The **year selector** at the top right sets which season the overview shows.

## What athletes see

**Athletes manage only their own goals** — there's no group switcher and no Entire Group overview, just their own season buckets. An athlete can view and edit their goals (including key results and attributes), leave comments, and add a final evaluation — even on a goal a coach created for them.

## How it connects

- Goals and their final evaluations are the spine of pre- and post-season review
  conversations.
- A goal carries **its own season** — that's what buckets the list. It isn't tied to a
  [Season Calendar](/en/plan/season-calendar) event or a [Plan](/en/plan/plan) week, so a goal
  can span whatever stretch of the season it needs to.
- **Verification** measures a goal against your team's **guidelines document** — a reference
  that lives outside Yarmill, behind **Open guidelines**.
