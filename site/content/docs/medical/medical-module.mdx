## Where to find Injuries & Illnesses

**Open the Medical module from the main menu.** As a **coach or medical staff member**, pick one of your groups and then an athlete, or choose **Entire Group** to see that squad at once. As an **athlete**, you go straight to your own records, if your team gives you access to them.

<Info>Medical data is sensitive, so who can see and edit it is **permission-controlled and
configured per team**, and also per group — a coach can have full access to one squad and
read-only access to another. If you don't see this module, it may not be enabled for your
team — ask your administrator.</Info>

## Who can train today

**The group overview is one row per athlete**, and it's the screen you'll use day to day. Each row carries:

- a **status pill** — the athlete's **worst** current **Injury status**, the field that says how much they can do right now, across everything they have open. Green means full training, which is not the same as an empty record: an athlete can be back in the session with an injury still open;
- their **open health problems**, each with an icon marking it as an injury or an illness;
- the **expected return to full training**, or a dash when no date has been set. A date that has passed turns red and says how many days overdue it is.

An athlete with nothing open still gets a row, marked **No health problems**.

## Log an injury or illness

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add the record">
    Select **+** above the athlete's record list, and choose **Injury** or **Illness**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name it">
    The record opens straight away, ready to edit. Type over the placeholder title.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in what you know">
    Set the **Injury status** first — it's the field the rest of Yarmill reads — then the
    dates, the diagnosis, the circumstances, a note, and whoever is looking after it. There's
    no Save: **everything auto-saves**, and every change is written to **Activity**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A new record starts with its **Date of injury** set to today, its **Treatment status** at **Open**, its **Classification** at **New**, and its **Injury status** at **Modified training** for you to correct.

### Or log it in seconds with quick entry

**New quick entry** in the tool bar — or the **N** shortcut — takes only the essentials: the
**Entry type**, a name, the **Status**, the **Date of injury**, and any **files** you already
have. It's the one to reach for pitch-side, when the point is to get the group screen right
today and fill in the clinical detail later.

It's also the one place in the module that waits for you: **Save** commits it, **Open full
detail** commits it and takes you into the record, and closing the dialog without either
discards it.

## What a record holds

Every field on a record, in the order the record shows them. The first four sit together in
the **Properties** row, the three dates under **Key Dates**.

<ParamField path="Name">
  What the problem is, in the athlete's record and in every list — for example, "Right ankle
  sprain (lateral)". Click the title to rename it.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Type">
  **Injury** or **Illness**, chosen when you create the record. It sets the icon on the card,
  and it decides which half of the diagnosis codelist you search.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Classification" default="New">
  **New** or **Recurring** — the flag that makes a repeat problem visible as a pattern rather
  than as another isolated entry.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Treatment status" default="Open">
  **Open** or **Closed**. Closing a record moves it to the Closed list.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Injury status" default="Modified training">
  How much the athlete can do right now: **Full training or competition** (green), **Modified
  training or competition** (amber), or **No training or competition** (red). This is the
  colour on the group overview.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Responsible staff">
  The staff member or institution looking after the problem. Free text.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Date of injury" default="Today">
  When the problem started.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Expected return to full training">
  When you expect the athlete back. This is the date the group overview shows, and the one it
  turns red once it has passed. Clear it with the **×** if it stops being meaningful.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Closure date">
  Stamped when you close the record, and editable afterwards.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Circumstances">
  Optional context for how it happened — **Activity, Injury mechanism, Location, Severity,
  Surface**. The values in each list are **configured for your team**: a football club might
  log mechanism, playing surface and severity from purpose-built lists, while another team has
  a different set or none at all.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Note">
  Free text — the mechanism, the treatment plan, what to watch for on the way back.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Diagnosis">
  The coded diagnosis, its **Side** (injuries only), and the body-region and structure tags
  that follow from it. See [Code the diagnosis](#code-the-diagnosis) below.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Files">
  Documents on the record: medical reports, X-ray and MRI images, physiotherapy plans.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Activity">
  The automatic change log, and the comment box. See
  [Track the return to training](#track-the-return-to-training).
</ParamField>

## Code the diagnosis

**Add a diagnosis from the record's Diagnosis section.** Type what you're looking for in plain
words — `ankle sprain`, `tonsillitis` — pick the entry from the list, set the **Side** if it's
an injury (an illness hasn't got one), and select **Add**, or press **Ctrl ↵** (**⌘ ↵** on a
Mac). Yarmill tags the record with the body region and structure that follow from the code, so
`AL1 Sprain lateral collateral ligament ankle` carries *ankle* and *ligament* without anyone
typing them.

The list is filtered by the record's type — injury codes for an injury, illness codes for an
illness — and the record links straight out to the full **OSIICS code list**.

<Info>A team can **pin the diagnoses it uses most** to the top of the list, so the everyday
cases are there before you type anything. Which ones are pinned is chosen by the team and
**configured for your instance** — it doesn't follow your usage on its own.</Info>

### What OSIICS is, and why the code matters

**OSIICS** — the **Orchard Sports Injury and Illness Classification System** — is one of the
two coding systems aligned with the International Olympic Committee's consensus statement on
recording injuries and illnesses in sport. Each entry is a short code and a plain-language
diagnosis, built so the code carries the anatomy: an injury code starts with the body region
(`AL1` is an ankle ligament), and illness codes all start with `M` (`MPIT` is tonsillitis).

**A coded diagnosis stays comparable; free text doesn't.** Ten people describing the same
problem produce ten different strings, and a season of them can't be added up. Coded, the same
problem reads the same years and staff changes later, a recurring ankle is recognisably the
same ankle, and what you hand to a federation means what they mean by it.

It codes **what the problem is**, not how it happened or how bad it was — that's
**Circumstances** and the key dates. And unlike the Circumstances lists, which are Yarmill's
own and configured per team, the codelist is the same on every instance. That's the point of a
standard. Its labels show in **English as well as Czech**; the code underneath doesn't change.

<Info>OSIICS is maintained by John Orchard and colleagues and is free to use with
acknowledgement, which Yarmill makes here. Yarmill's codelist is **OSIICS version 16**; the
codelist and its documentation are at
[johnorchard.com/osiics.html](https://www.johnorchard.com/osiics.html), and the current
version is published open access in JSAMS Plus
([doi:10.1016/j.jsampl.2025.100119](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsampl.2025.100119)).</Info>

## Track the return to training

**Keep the Injury status current** — it's the field the rest of Yarmill reads for availability. A
typical recovery walks it back up: **No training or competition** while the athlete is out,
**Modified training or competition** as they start doing some of the session, **Full training or
competition** when they're available again. Move the **Expected return to full training** as the picture changes rather than leaving a
date to go red.

**Every change is logged automatically** under **Activity**, with who made it and when — the
status changes, the dates, the diagnosis, each circumstance. Use the comment box to add what
the log can't infer: how the session went, what the consultant said.

## Close a record, and the history it leaves

**When a problem resolves, set its treatment status to Closed.** Yarmill stamps the **closure
date**, moves the record to the **Closed** list, and sets its **Injury status** to **Full
training or competition** — so the athlete's availability updates without a second step.

A record that turns out not to be over can be **reopened**: set its treatment status back to
**Open** and it returns to the open list.

Closed records stay in the athlete's **Closed** list, so the next flare-up of the same problem
can be marked **Recurring** against something you can point at — the basis for preventing the
next one rather than only managing this one.

## Delete a record

**Select the trash icon in the tool bar** and confirm.

<Warning>Deleting is permanent, and it takes the diagnosis, the notes, the files and the
history with it.</Warning>

A problem that turned out to be nothing should be **closed**, not deleted — the record of what
was suspected and ruled out is worth keeping.

## What athletes see

**Where their team allows it, athletes work with their own records** — their Open and Closed
lists, the full detail of each one, and **New quick entry** to add a problem themselves after a
doctor's visit, an X-ray, or an MRI.

**On other instances the module isn't shown to athletes at all.** Which one you have is a
configured permission, so check your own setup before telling a squad to keep their records up
to date.

## How it connects

- The **Injury status** flows into [Team daily readiness](/en/analytics/analytics), where
  it sits alongside the day's [wellness questionnaire](/en/reality/wellness-questionnaire) and
  device recovery from [integrations](/en/platform/integrations), so the morning overview
  already knows who's out — see [Read team readiness](/en/tutorials/read-readiness).
- Illness and injury periods are shaded into the background of the
  [recovery and training-load charts](/en/analytics/analytics), so a dip in load or recovery is
  read against when the athlete was actually hurt.
- The **Expected return to full training** is a planning input, not a clearance: it tells you
  when to start rebuilding load, not that the athlete is available.
- Documents added to a record are stored with it, alongside the athlete's other
  [files](/en/platform/files).
