## Where to find Injuries & Illnesses

**Open the Medical module from the main menu.** As a **coach or medical staff member**, pick one of your groups and then an athlete, or choose **Entire Group** to see that squad at once. As an **athlete**, you go straight to your own records, if your team gives you access to them.

<Info>Medical data is sensitive, so who can see and edit it is **permission-controlled and
configured per team**. Some teams give athletes full access to their own records, others keep
the module to coaches and medical staff. Access is also set per group, so a coach can have
full access to one squad and read-only access to another. If you don't see this module, it may
not be enabled for your team — ask your administrator.</Info>

## Who can train today

**The group overview is one row per athlete**, and it's the screen you'll use day to day. Each row carries:

- a **status pill** — the athlete's **worst** current training limitation across everything they have open, so a green row means nothing is holding them back;
- their **open health problems**, each with an icon marking it as an injury or an illness;
- the **expected return to full training**, or a dash when no date has been set. A date that has passed turns red and says how many days overdue it is.

An athlete with nothing open still gets a row, marked **No health problems**.

## Log an injury or illness

The fastest path is **New quick entry** — enough to make the group overview right today, with the clinical detail added later.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a new entry">
    Select **New quick entry** in the tool bar — or just press **N**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Capture the essentials">
    Choose **Injury** or **Illness**, name it, set the **training limitation** and the
    **date**, and attach any **files** you already have.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save it, or go straight to the detail">
    Quick entry is the one place in the module that waits for you: nothing exists until you
    **Save**. **Open full detail** takes you to the full record to keep filling it in.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### What a record holds

<ParamField path="Title & type">
  What the problem is, marked as an **injury** or an **illness** when you create it.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Classification" default="New">
  **New** or **Recurring** — the flag that makes a repeat problem visible as a pattern rather
  than as another isolated entry.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Treatment status" default="Open">
  **Open** or **Closed**. Closing a record moves it to the Closed list.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Training limitation">
  Labelled **Injury Status** on the record. How much the athlete can do right now: **Full training or competition** (green),
  **Modified training or competition** (amber), or **No training or competition** (red). This
  is the color on the group overview, and the value other modules read.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Key dates">
  The **start date**, the **expected return to full training** — the date the overview shows
  and flags when it passes — and, once the record is closed, a **closure date**.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Diagnosis">
  The coded diagnosis, its **side**, and the body-region and structure tags that follow from
  it. See
  [Code the diagnosis](#code-the-diagnosis) below.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Circumstances">
  Optional context for how it happened — **activity, injury mechanism, location, severity,
  surface**. The values in each list are **configured for your team**: a football club might
  log mechanism, playing surface and severity from purpose-built lists, while another team has
  a different set or none at all.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Note">
  Free text — the mechanism, the treatment plan, what to watch for on the way back.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Files">
  Documents on the record: medical reports, X-ray and MRI images, physiotherapy plans.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Responsible staff">
  The staff member or institution looking after the problem.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="Activity">
  The automatic change log, and the comment box. See
  [Track the return to training](#track-the-return-to-training).
</ParamField>

## Code the diagnosis

**Add a diagnosis from the record's Diagnosis section.** Type what you're looking for in plain
words — `ankle sprain`, `tonsillitis` — pick the matching entry from the list, set the **side**
(left, right, bilateral, or unknown), then select **Add** (or press **Ctrl ↵**) to attach it.
Yarmill tags the record with the body region
and structure that follow from the code, so `AL1 Sprain lateral collateral ligament ankle`
carries *ankle* and *ligament* without anyone typing them.

**The list you search is filtered by the record's type**: an injury record searches injury
codes, an illness record searches illness codes. The record also links straight out to the
**OSIICS code list**, for when you want to read around a code rather than search for one.

team uses most sit at the top of the list, ahead of the full codelist. Which ones those are is
**configured for your team**.</Info>

### What OSIICS is, and why the code matters

The codelist is **OSIICS** — the **Orchard Sports Injury and Illness Classification System**,
one of the two coding systems recognised by the International Olympic Committee for recording
injuries and illnesses in sport (the other is the Sports Medicine Diagnostic Coding System),
and one of the most widely used in injury surveillance. Each entry is a short code and a
plain-language diagnosis: the first character of an injury code is the body region (`A` ankle,
`K` knee), the next says what tissue is involved, and illness codes all begin with `M`, then an
organ system.

**A coded diagnosis is countable; free text isn't.** Ten staff describing the same problem in
their own words produce ten different strings, and the season's worth of records can't be
added up. Coded, they can be counted — hamstring injuries this season against last, whether
one athlete's ankle keeps coming back, how your squad compares with published figures for the
sport. Because the region is the first character, detailed codes roll up to region-level counts
without anyone re-coding anything.

**OSIICS codes what the problem is, not how it happened or how bad it was** — mechanism,
severity, surface and activity are the record's **Circumstances**, and time lost is the key
dates.

Unlike the Circumstances lists — which are Yarmill's own, and configured per team — the
diagnosis codelist is **the same on every Yarmill instance**. That's the point of a standard.
Yarmill shows its labels in **Czech as well as English**; the OSIICS code underneath is the
same either way.

<Info>OSIICS is maintained by John Orchard and colleagues and is free to use with
acknowledgement, which Yarmill makes here. The codelist and its documentation are at
[johnorchard.com/osiics.html](https://www.johnorchard.com/osiics.html), and the current version
is published open access in JSAMS Plus
([doi:10.1016/j.jsampl.2025.100119](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsampl.2025.100119)).</Info>

## Track the return to training

**Keep the training limitation current** — it's the field the rest of Yarmill reads for availability. A
typical recovery walks it back up: **No training or competition** while the athlete is out,
**Modified training or competition** as they start doing some of the session, **Full training or
competition** when they're available again. Move the **expected return** as the picture changes
rather than leaving a date to go red.

**Every change is logged automatically** under **Activity**, with who made it and when, and
related edits are grouped so a run of changes to one record reads as a block instead of a wall
of lines. Use the comment box to add what the log can't infer — how the session went, what the
consultant said.

## Close a record, and the history it leaves

**When a problem resolves, set its treatment status to Closed.** Yarmill stamps the **closure
date**, moves the record to the **Closed** list, and sets its training limitation to **Full
training or competition** — so the athlete's availability updates without a second step.

Closed records stay in the athlete's **Closed** list, so the next flare-up of the same problem
can be marked **Recurring** against something you can point at — the basis for preventing the
next one rather than only managing this one.

## Delete a record

**Select the bin in the tool bar** and confirm.

<Warning>Deleting is permanent, and it takes the diagnosis, the notes, the files and the
history with it.</Warning>

A problem that turned out to be nothing should be **closed**, not deleted — the record of what
was suspected and ruled out is worth keeping.

## What athletes see

**Where their team allows it, athletes work with their own records** — their Open and Closed
lists, the full detail of each one, and **New quick entry** to add a problem themselves after a
doctor's visit, an X-ray, or an MRI. They see only their own; there's no group and no other
athletes.

**On other instances the module isn't shown to athletes at all**, and some setups sit between
the two — visible, but read-only. Which one you have is a configured permission, so check your
own setup before telling a squad to keep their records up to date.

## How it connects

- The **training limitation** flows into [Team daily readiness](/en/analytics/analytics), so the
  morning overview already knows who's out — see
  [Read team readiness](/en/tutorials/read-readiness).
- Illness and injury periods are shaded into the background of the
  [recovery and training-load charts](/en/analytics/analytics), so a dip in load or recovery is
  read against when the athlete was actually hurt.
- An **expected-return date** is a planning input, not a clearance: it tells you when to start
  rebuilding load, not that the athlete is available.
- Documents added to a record are stored with it, alongside the athlete's other
  [files](/en/platform/files).
- [Yollanda](/en/platform/yollanda) reads the module, so a question about an athlete's injury
  history or current availability is answered from the records rather than from training data
  alone.
- The training limitation sits alongside the other readiness inputs — the day's
  [wellness questionnaire](/en/reality/wellness-questionnaire) and device recovery from
  [integrations](/en/platform/integrations).
